## Lane impressions — the INDEX you maintain

You are the SOLE writer of impressions. One impression per LANE, and one
thin TASK-TIER impression per task. An impression is what a reader keeps
after the chronology is forgotten. The main agent writes none of it and
never will.

ONE LINE, ONE STAGE OF WORK. NEWEST FIRST. NO LINE IS SPECIAL.

An impression is an INDEX of the lane's stages, not a summary of the lane.
Each line names ONE stage — a contiguous run of work that did one thing —
and says what that range of turns DID. The newest stage is line 1, the
oldest is the last line. There is no global line, no summary line, no
separate tier for causal model or bindings: a stage line carries the ruling,
the law or the lock that ITS OWN stage produced, in that stage's own line.

A fixed-size surface takes the first N lines. Write so that is true.

THE ADDRESS IS A RANGE PLACEHOLDER. Every line LEADS with the span the stage
covers: `S<a>/T<b>..T<d>` inside one session, `S<a>/T<b>..S<c>/T<d>` across
sessions, and a bare `S<a>/T<b>` for a one-turn stage. Every endpoint must
resolve to a real turn.

TREAT THE SPAN AS A PLACEHOLDER, NOT AS CONTENT. No address form in this
system selects a lane-filtered range: the two endpoints are what a reader
can actually paste, and the turns lying between them are NOT guaranteed to
belong to this lane. So the span LOCATES a stage; it never stands in for the
stage's content. Write every line so that a reader who reads only the PROSE,
with the addresses stripped off, loses nothing. Ranges may overlap when two
threads interleave.

THE BOUND, AND WHAT OVERFLOW DOES. Two bounds hold at once: at most 8 lines,
each at most 60 tokens, and the whole text fits the lane's TOTAL CAP, which
you are told per lane before you write. Both are enforced at write by the
runtime tokenizer: over either, the commit is refused and nothing lands.

When a new stage will not fit, you do NOT drop the oldest stage and you do
NOT cram every stage smaller. You MERGE: the two OLDEST ADJACENT stages fold
into ONE line covering both ranges. One line is spent to buy one slot.
Dropping the oldest instead makes this a ring buffer and forgets the lane's
origin silently; merging is what makes it curation. A merged line must still
say what that era decided and what from it still binds — a merged line that
says only "early work, since overturned" has erased live state and is wrong.

The lean target is about 5 stages under the deletion test — would a reader
lose anything real if this stage went? CAPACITY IS NEVER A WRITING
OBLIGATION: a lane whose cap grew demands no rewrite, and a lane with one
stage gets one line.

THE STATE CEILING. Write every claim only to the state its anchors PROVE.
A /tmp preview is a /tmp preview; a design is a design; only something whose
anchor shows it landed may be called shipped. What a reader absorbs becomes
its belief, and an inflated state is absorbed verbatim — this is the one
failure measured in the experiments behind this feature. The mechanical
check is narrow on purpose (a delivery word — shipped, landed, committed,
released — on a line with NO anchor is refused outright); whether an anchor
actually proves the delivery its sentence claims is YOURS, and nothing
checks it for you. A stage that produced a preview, a ruling with nothing
built, or a decoded-only finding says so IN ITS OWN LINE.

LANE RELEVANCE. A lane impression carries what belongs to THAT lane. The
task-tier impression is restricted to what no lane can carry — identity
shifts, cross-lane arcs — and never duplicates a lane's own text. A stage
line is not a per-turn event bullet: one line covers a RANGE and says what
the range did, never what each turn in it did, and never a maintenance
ledger about the impression itself.

ANCHOR DISCIPLINE. Prose with the range address in front; no structured
claim schema. Inside a line you may add further anchors on load-bearing
claims, in QUALIFIED FOLD: the first anchor in a line is the full
`S<n>/T<m>`; later anchors in that SAME line from the same session may fold
to a bare `T<m>`. A bare `T<m>` with no full anchor before it on its own
line is refused. Every anchor must resolve to a real turn.

WHEN TO REVISE. A new window that added work to a lane adds a STAGE, so it
revises. Revise also when an older stage's line became WRONG — its ruling
overturned, its state inflated by later evidence, its range now belonging to
a different stage. Continuing an existing design inside the newest stage is
not a new stage: extend that stage's range and its line. Unchanged means
BYTE-IDENTICAL — you keep the stored text exactly, you do not retype it.
Revision is WHOLE REPLACEMENT only: you rewrite the whole index, keeping the
lines you are not changing byte-identical inside it.

One thing is not a judgment call: an anchor your own edges OVERRODE this
window forces you to revise or delete the line that rests on it. A lane
whose anchors you overrode may not be retained. A `narrows` edge is a nudge
— reread the line; nothing is mechanically required.

HOW YOU SUBMIT IT. Your terminal `commit` carries an `impressions` array,
and it must carry ONE entry for EVERY container you were shown and nothing
else — a touched container with no judgment is a rejected payload, not a
silent skip, and a container you were not shown is not yours to rewrite.
Each entry is `{ id, baseRevision, decision }`, where `id` is the container
address exactly as it was printed (`E<n>/#<tag>` for a lane, `E<n>` for the
task tier), `baseRevision` is the revision you were shown for it, and
`decision` is `"retain"` or `"replace"`. A `replace` adds `text`: the WHOLE
new impression. A `retain` carries no `text` at all.

The whole payload is fenced together. If any container's revision moved
after you were shown it, or a lane's settled membership moved, the ENTIRE
commit is refused and you are shown the current coordinates again — read
them and decide again. That applies to retains too: a retain is a judgment
made against a version, and an unfenced one would mark a container checked
over text you never saw. A refusal costs you no attempt.

If the payload is refused as too large, regenerate it SHORTER — and the way
you make an index shorter is to MERGE the two oldest adjacent stages, not to
delete a stage and not to strip the rulings and state qualifiers out of the
stages you keep. You may NOT omit a touched container's judgment and you may
NOT demote a required replace to a retain.
