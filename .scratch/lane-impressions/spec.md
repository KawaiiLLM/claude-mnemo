## Problem Statement

Milestone nodes are discrete and unorganized: even a well-elected row block leaves the reader to reconstruct the arc, and blind tests measured the consequence — agents route drills accurately yet misidentify the project, because identity lives on the arc across nodes, not in any node. Meanwhile the segment card's narrative fields (done, decisions, next_steps) duplicate compact summaries and git history, and their maintenance burdens the main agent. Missing is what a human keeps after the chronology is forgotten: a per-lane IMPRESSION — why the line exists, what understanding governs it, what binds, what is proven and what hangs — synthesized across nodes, anchored for drilling.

## Solution

Settlement maintains one impression per lane (and a thin task-tier impression per segment): prefix-readable LINES, each a cross-node model claim, stored up to 500 tokens. **In this spec, LANE impressions render in exactly one place — the canonical lane route `recall(id="E<n>/#<tag>")` shows the full impression at the head of page 1 — and the TASK-TIER impression renders where it lives, the segment card's content slot.** The milestone block, the timeline lane view, and the election machinery are untouched (T2253): a fresh deployment has no impressions, so whether impressions ever replace the block's elected rows is decided later by a comparison test on ORGANICALLY-GROWN production impressions — this spec builds the organism, not the verdict. The segment card slims on the ruled schedule (backfill first, retire after) and keeps a mechanical pointer to the new surface.

Evidence of record, at its honest strength (peer): hand-written impressions matched the best elected arm (9.0, routing-corrected rubric) on ONE corpus and battery; a four-window chained generation experiment held the state ceiling at the /tmp-preview trap and flushed override-invalidated claims; the three-arm isolation showed pure re-lineation non-inferior (B=A=9.0) while the unreviewed writer STILL inflated a preview to shipped (C=8.5) and the blind reader absorbed the inflation verbatim. That last fact is why auto-maintenance and delivery-state discipline are ACCEPTANCE GATES below, not accomplished facts.

## User Stories

1. As a session agent drilling a lane via `recall(id="E<n>/#<tag>")`, I want its impression at the head of the results, so that I inherit the lane's mental model before its discrete rows.
2. As a session agent, I want an impression's first line to be the self-contained GLOBAL IMPRESSION (the lane's whole shape: what it is, its law, its state), so that any surface taking exactly line 1 — and any skimming reader — is never misled.
3. As a session agent reading a full impression, I want lines ordered by newcomer loss (global impression / causal model / bindings / frontier), so that the deep model reads in descending importance.
4. As a fresh session agent, I want every claim written only to the state its anchors prove, so that a /tmp preview never reads as shipped — what I absorb becomes my belief.
5. As a session agent, I want anchors on load-bearing claims in qualified fold form, so that any claim is drillable without a search.
6. As a session agent, I want impressions revised by whole replacement whenever a settlement window changes a lane's understanding, so that standing text never asserts overridden law.
7. As a session agent, I want an untouched understanding to keep its impression byte-identical, so that unchanged text stays diff-readable.
8. As a session agent, I want impressions free of per-turn event bullets, so that the surface never regresses to discrete rows.
9. As the user, I want settlement to own ALL impression writing — lane tier and task tier — so that the main agent carries zero impression and zero retired-field maintenance (goal/constraints/reference/insight stay its own).
10. As the user, I want the terminal settlement commit to durably carry its impression obligations, so that a crash after `done` cannot leave a dormant lane asserting stale law forever.
11. As the user, I want the milestone block, timeline lane view, and election machinery untouched, so that the replacement question is answered later by comparison on organic data, not by this spec.
12. As the user, I want backfill-seeded and settlement-maintained impressions distinguishable in storage, so that the future comparison test can select organically-grown samples mechanically.
13. As the user, I want the segment card slimmed only AFTER backfill seeds the successor surface, so that narrative content is never deleted before its new home holds it.
14. As a session agent reading the slimmed card, I want a mechanical pointer to the impression surface, so that the interim (no block display) does not orphan the narrative.
15. As a maintainer, I want the write-time validator's deterministic tier separated from settlement's semantic duties, so that lint coverage is never mistaken for state-ceiling coverage.
16. As a maintainer, I want manual lane lifecycle operations (declare/rename/merge, task merge) to leave a durable impression-maintenance debt, so that an impression cannot silently outlive or predate its lane's shape.
17. As a maintainer, I want the fixed-size form to be exactly line 1 for each lane a surface ADMITS (the multi-depth fitter is DEAD by ruling B; lane-level admission under a budget is a separate, undecided question for the future block ticket), so that one text-derivation rule serves every surface without smuggling an all-lanes coverage promise.
18. As a maintainer, I want the legacy backfill to be a distinct migration job, so that ordinary settlement never has to fake authority over content it cannot see.

## Implementation Decisions

### Storage

- The lane registry row gains: the impression text; a REVISION counter that is first an OPTIMISTIC-CONCURRENCY FENCE and only secondarily bookkeeping (peer round-2 finding 1) — every writer records the revision it READ, and its replacement carries that base revision; an ORIGIN mark distinguishing `backfill` from `settlement` — the future comparison test's eligibility is mechanical: at least one real touched-window replacement after backfill; a STALE flag on the lane row (set by lane merge) and symmetrically on the segment's task-tier impression (set by task merge) — each cleared only by a qualified CAS rewrite (see lifecycle debts).
- The segment gains the task-tier impression in its content field under the same storage rules, fence, and origin mark.
- Line form: newline-delimited, at most 8 lines. **LINE 1 IS THE GLOBAL IMPRESSION (USER RULED, T2260-T2261)**: one self-contained line, ≤150 tokens (and ≤ the lane's total cap where that binds tighter), carrying the lane's whole shape — what it is, its governing law, its current state — written to stand ALONE as the injection form: any surface that wants a fixed-size impression takes exactly line 1, no prefix arithmetic, no second maintained text (the dual-impression alternative was rejected as a drift farm: a derivable form is computed, never stored twice). Lines 2+ cap 60 tokens each and deepen the model (causal detail, bindings, frontier), ordered by newcomer loss as the deep model's reading order (no future surface cuts below line 1 — ruling B killed the prefix fitter). All caps enforced at write by the runtime tokenizer.
- **Absolute per-lane total cap (USER RULED, T2259-T2261, replacing the proportional form whose floor degenerated at high lane counts — E60's 33 lanes would ALL floor at 100 — and whose caps were non-local)**: `cap_lane = clamp(10 × settledMembers_lane, 100, 500)` — integer arithmetic, no denominator, no division-by-zero shape, LOCAL (only this lane's own membership moves its cap; foreign lane activity cannot). **Member-set definition (peer rounds 4-5)**: `settledMembers_lane` is the SETTLED, CANONICAL, ERA-SCOPED lane universe SHARED WITH the frontier section and the lane-adjacency view (the settlement-covered truth via the shared coverage reader) — explicitly NOT the recall content route's member index, which deliberately keeps rewound members visible for readers and is gated by no settlement truth; computed as the POST-COMMIT PROJECTION (the committing window's own members count; a first replacement must not systematically undercount). A brand-new lane has 0 members → cap = 100 (the clamp floor; no special case). The cap binds REPLACEMENTS only: retained text is never force-trimmed; a replace fits the cap current at its write. The lean target stays ~5 lines under the deletion test; capacity is never a writing obligation (a grown cap demands no rewrite).
- The task-tier impression keeps a flat 500 cap and the same line-1 global-impression rule (≤150).
- Prose with inline anchors; no structured claim schema. Anchors in qualified fold: first anchor in a line full `S<n>/T<m>`, later same-session anchors in the same line fold to `T<m>`.

### Settlement maintenance — lane tier

- Settlement is the SOLE writer. The stage-2 run, after edge adjudication, decides per touched lane: `retain` or `replace(full impression)` — whole-impression replacement only (prefix-readability couples lines; partial patches leave half-new states).
- **Durable obligation (peer-ruled)**: the terminal commit payload carries the impression replacements, validated inside the terminal transaction — `done` never swallows an unpersisted maintenance obligation. The outbox alternative stays rejected unless the payload measurement (gates below) shows in-transaction TEXT updates heavy.
- **Freshness fence (peer rounds 2-3: covers RETAIN as well as replace)**: the terminal payload carries `{baseRevision, decision: retain|replace}` for EVERY touched lane and the task tier — a retain is a semantic judgment made against the version read, and an unfenced retain would falsely ACK debts and mark lanes checked over text it never saw. The transaction CAS-checks the FULL touched set first; any mismatch (a concurrent job or a manual lifecycle write moved any row) rejects the WHOLE commit for re-read-re-decide. Replacements update text+revision; retains touch nothing but must pass the same fence. Turn-field write-gate philosophy: another writer after your read is a hard stop, never a silent overwrite.
- **Cap coordinates share the fence (peer round-4 finding 2)**: the writer receives an ADVISORY CAP computed from its own frozen member snapshot (the staged settlement's persisted per-lane member snapshots are the reuse target) — the model must know its budget BEFORE generating, not discover at commit that 450 tokens face a 135 cap. The payload carries each touched lane's membership generation (and member count); the terminal transaction recomputes the cap on the post-commit projection with the SAME integer formula and CAS-checks the membership generation — a stage-1 or tag write that moved the lane's membership without touching the impression row rejects the commit for re-read-re-decide (the impression revision alone cannot see membership drift).
- **Touched-lane set**: lanes with window members; tail lanes of edges written/retracted this run; HEAD lanes of those edges; lanes named by consumed lifecycle debts (below). The anchor-invalidation check runs UNCONDITIONALLY for every touched lane: an anchor overridden by a new edge forces revise-or-delete of its sentence; a `narrows` hit nudges, never mechanically deletes.
- **Unchanged guard**: revise only when existence reason, causal model, bindings, evidence state, or open boundaries changed; continuing an existing design is not a change; unchanged = byte-identical retain.
- **Lifecycle debts (peer round-2 finding 2: a real mechanism, not the ledger metaphor — the existing touch ledger is job-scoped and cannot host job-less writes)**: a NEW durable debt table keyed by qualified `(task, lane?)` identity. Manual remember operations — lane declare/rename/merge, task merge/retag — atomically insert a debt in the same transaction as the operation. Debt semantics: a RENAME re-keys its debts to the new tag; a MERGE leaves only the survivor's key; claim/ack discipline — a settlement run CLAIMS the debts of tasks it is attached to at claim time, folds them into its touched set, and ACKS them only in its successful terminal commit (a failed run's claims release for retry; consumption is never read-and-delete). Eligibility: only a run whose session is attached to the debt's task may claim it — a debt with no eligible run WAITS DURABLY (visible on no reader surface unless it is merge-family STALE, whose marker rule is below).
- **Merge staleness — AMENDED BY USER RULING T2269 (ticket 07); the amended text governs**: 「直接把两个印象合并，然后等下次重写。合并没有上限，但重写还是有 500 上限」. **A FOLD CONCATENATES.** When two containers fold into one, their impressions are joined into the SURVIVOR — the survivor's text first (its identity leads), then the folded one's, separated by ONE newline — and left READABLE. Either side empty degenerates to the other's exact bytes (an empty survivor carries the folded side over unchanged, ORIGIN INCLUDED); both empty leaves NULL. A force-merge folding several lanes concatenates each fold in turn. **The concatenation is UNCAPPED** — no validator run, no line-count check, no truncation at fold time; `cap_lane` binds settlement REPLACEMENTS only, exactly as it already declines to force-trim retained text. This reaches the LANE MERGE, the LANE RENAME (mint-then-fold, so the impression, its revision and its origin survive the relabel instead of dying with the old row) and the TASK TIER (symmetrically, and ONLY when both sides' `impression_origin` is non-null — a phase-1 task whose `content` is still legacy field text is not an impression and keeps the ordinary prose merge). The fold MOVES the CAS fence on the survivor: a concurrent run's `replace` decided against the pre-fold text may not land. **STALE keeps its forcing job and loses its display job.** A lane MERGE still sets the survivor's flag in the merge transaction; a TASK MERGE still sets the surviving task-tier impression's; a force-merge still STALEs each folded survivor lane with its own debt. The flag now means exactly one thing — **this container MUST BE REWRITTEN**: settlement refuses a `retain` over it, and the flag clears only when a qualified run CAS-rewrites. It no longer suppresses anything; the `[impression pending synthesis]` status line is RETIRED (Rev 8's suppression traded a real loss — the material — against a speculative one, and the ruling chose the readable stale join: concatenated prose is stale, not misleading). A deleted source row's impression dies with the row ONLY where no fold carried it — the folded side's text has already been joined onto the survivor by then. Non-merge debts (declare/rename/retag) WAIT DURABLY — they do not falsify existing prose; only the merge family, which fuses two identities, sets STALE.

### Settlement maintenance — task tier (peer finding 4: the full contract)

- The task-tier impression is restricted to what no lane can carry (identity shifts, cross-lane arcs) under an explicit non-duplication rule.
- **Task touched when**: any of its lanes is touched; a cross-lane edge lands between its lanes; a task merge/retag debt names it. Same retain/replace protocol, same unchanged guard, same unconditional anchor invalidation, same caps and validator, and the replacement rides the SAME terminal transaction as the lane replacements.
- Its initial content comes from the migration job (below); thereafter ordinary settlement maintains it. The main agent never writes it.

### Legacy backfill — a distinct migration job with a lifecycle and phases (peer round-2 finding 3)

- Ordinary settlement deliberately cannot see segment fields and holds no whole-task context; it is NOT the backfill author. The backfill is a separate migration JOB per task: inputs = the retiring field contents (done/decisions/next_steps + current content), the task's full declared-lane roster, and each lane's member/anchor index; outputs = one atomic batch of per-lane initial impressions + the task-tier impression, all `origin=backfill`; the task's old fields are cleared IN the same committing transaction, never before.
- **Job lifecycle**: durable rows with pending/claimed/done/failed states, bounded retry, idempotent re-run (a re-claimed job re-reads and re-generates; the atomic batch makes half-migrated tasks impossible). A model job cannot run inside a schema migration — it runs asynchronously after deployment.
- **Source-snapshot fence (peer round-3 finding 2)**: Phase 1 keeps the old fields WRITABLE, so the job's inputs can move under it — a decision appended mid-generation must never be swallowed by a stale-snapshot clear. The migration payload carries and the committing transaction CAS-checks ALL source inputs: the four retiring fields' write sequences (or content hashes), the task/lane registry generation, and the member/anchor snapshot generation. Any movement rejects the atomic commit; the job re-reads and regenerates. The impression revision alone is insufficient (first backfill predates any impression row). Fixtures: a field append landing mid-model-call; a lane merge landing mid-model-call. Cap-universe fixture (peer round-5 finding 2): one lane holding settled, unsettled, and rewound members — only the settled canonical era-scoped count feeds the cap.
- **Coverage (peer round-3 finding 5)**: backfill covers EVERY task carrying legacy fields, open AND closed — closed tasks stay recallable and can reopen, so history must migrate too; the old field enums leave the tool only after ALL such tasks have cut over.
- **Two deployment phases (pinned)**: Phase 1 ships schema + debt/job tables + the new write path; old fields remain fully readable and writable, cards unchanged. Phase 2 is PER-TASK: when a task's backfill job commits, THAT task's card switches to the slimmed form with the pointer line. The remember tool's old field enums and teaching are removed only in a LATER release, after every task carrying legacy fields has cut over — a deleted enum before a completed backfill would strand unmaintainable fields.
- **Unresolved content refuses cutover (peer round-2 finding 4 — the residual-note container is DEAD)**: content that maps to no lane and is not task-level does NOT get a new home invented for it; the job reports `unresolved`, the source fields stay UNCLEARED, and that task's cutover is refused until a human or a better mapping resolves it. No third tier, no silent discard.
- **Anchor law for migration (same finding)**: load-bearing claims MUST be re-sourced to qualified anchors through the member/anchor index — most legacy field rows carry no addresses, and migrating them anchor-less would legally mint untraceable current law. A load-bearing claim that cannot be anchored goes to `unresolved`, never into a live impression.
- The migration mode gets its own teaching variant (synthesizes from fields, not windows) on the same law (line form, state ceiling, checklist).
- **Scale gate**: the largest task among ALL carrying legacy fields (open or closed — determined by QUERY at migration time, not inferred from the live roster; E60 is today's likely candidate) is measured through the migration path — input assembly size, model context, output batch, transaction time — before the phase-2 rollout.

### Validator (write-time, in-transaction; honest two-tier split — peer finding 6)

- **Deterministic tier (code, rejects the transaction)**: per-line/line-1/line-count/total caps; anchor-format regex (qualified fold); anchor resolvability via the existing citation validation; a delivery-class word (shipped/landed/committed/released) with NO anchor on its line; malformed structure.
- **Semantic duties (teaching, NOT code, never called mechanical)**: the state ceiling itself — whether a resolvable anchor actually proves delivery is semantic; the code validator does NOT reject a resolvable-but-insufficient anchor. Sequence-word soft lint warns, never rejects.
- **The three delivery cases live in three DIFFERENT test classes (peer's split, verbatim)**: (a) unit — delivery word with no/malformed anchor → transaction rejects; (b) generation acceptance — a writer shown `/tmp preview only` source must not submit shipped-class wording (graded regeneration gate, not a code test); (c) lifecycle — a delivery anchor later overridden forces replacement at the next touching settlement (a maintenance test, not a payload-validator test).

### Display — the canonical lane route (peer finding 2: the filter route is dead)

- **The ONE display surface: `recall(id="E<n>/#<tag>")`** — it already uniquely resolves `(task, tag)`, validates the declaration, scopes results to the lane's members, and writes the lane-read receipt. **Pagination pinned to the SIMPLE option (peer round-2 finding 5)**: the impression is a FIXED PREFACE on page 1 only, OUTSIDE the existing member paginator — the member pagination mechanics are untouched, and no new budget algorithm is designed. **GROWTH PROMISE, AMENDED BY USER RULING T2269 (ticket 07): EXACTLY THE STORED BYTES, SPLICED IN FRONT** (plus the one blank line separating preface from page), NOT "at most the 500-token cap" — the cap binds settlement replacements, and a fold concatenates without one, so a lane folded N times before a settlement run reaches it renders up to N+1 cap-sized texts. Deeper pages carry no repeat; a lane without an impression renders no placeholder; **a STALE container renders its stored text like any other — no suppression, no marker** (ticket 07 retired `[impression pending synthesis]` together with the branch that produced it; a fold always leaves text unless both sides were empty, which is the render-nothing case). Display ownership, stated once: the LANE preface belongs to the `E<n>/#<tag>` route's page 1; the TASK-TIER impression belongs to the segment card's content slot — the card is the task tier's display surface, the lane route is the lane tier's.
- `filter.tag` queries render NO impression: the filter is a global exact-match that cannot bind a qualified lane, and a mixed result set under one lane's impression would misattribute (peer's analysis adopted; the user's 指定泳道 is honestly implemented by the canonical route).
- The milestone block, timeline lane view, attach receipt, and election machinery are UNTOUCHED.
- **The multi-depth fitter is DEAD (ruling B); lane ADMISSION is not ruled**: any fixed-size surface takes EXACTLY line 1 for each lane it ADMITS — no depth rounds, no prefix-closure rule, no over-the-gap fixture, no line-2+ budget arithmetic. WHICH lanes a budget admits (33 line-1s ≥3300 tokens cannot all enter a 2000 block) and in what order is the future block-replacement ticket's open question — it may reuse, retire, or replace the election there; this spec does not rule it. The digest/tag floor remains the ONLY full-vocabulary guarantee. Lines 2+ serve the FULL render on the lane route; newcomer-loss order is a reading convention.

### Segment card slimming (sequenced; peer finding 3's bridge)

- Order is law (peer round-2 small closure — the pointer precedes retirement): (1) the migration job seeds a task's impressions; (2) the card gains the mechanical pointer line — `lane impressions: recall(id="E<n>/#<tag>")` — and the tool teaching names the new surface; (3) only then do that task's done/decisions/next_steps retire and content become the task-tier impression. goal/constraints/reference/insight keep; no vocabulary expansion on the pointer (tags still come from the milestone digest). The old field enums leave the remember tool in a later release (deployment phase 2's tail).

## Testing Decisions

- Two seams (user-confirmed shape; display seam renamed per the re-ruling): the settlement TERMINAL COMMIT surface and the RECALL LANE-ROUTE output. Generation-quality gates are experiment-fixture acceptance, not a code seam.
  1. **Commit surface**: payload-carried lane + task-tier replacements land in one transaction (crash-shaped test: a failed commit leaves neither edges nor impressions; success leaves both); **CAS fence fixtures — two jobs read v1, first commits v2, the second's ENTIRE terminal commit rejects and re-decides; a manual lifecycle write between a run's read and commit likewise rejects it; A retains at v1 while B replaced to v2 → A's commit rejects (the retain fence)**; deterministic-tier rejections one property per fixture (incl. the no-anchor delivery word); touched sets incl. edge-HEAD lanes and task-touch conditions; unconditional anchor invalidation; unchanged guard byte-identity; **debt lifecycle — insert-with-operation atomicity, rename re-key, merge survivor-key, claim/ack with a failed run releasing its claims, attachment eligibility (an unattached run cannot claim)**; lane merge AND task merge set STALE in their transactions (task-tier symmetric; force-merge folded lanes each STALE+debt) and a qualified CAS-rewrite clears them; the `[impression pending synthesis]` status line renders while stale and cannot leak old prose; origin marks; the payload cap's deterministic rejection routing to regeneration-only retry then operator-visible failure. Prior art: settlement direct-write and staged-settlement suites.
  2. **Recall lane route**: impression as a page-1-only fixed preface outside the member paginator (deeper pages unchanged; response ≤ status quo + 500 tok); absent impression = no placeholder; STALE = old prose suppressed AND the `[impression pending synthesis]` marker asserted (lane recall AND the card's task-tier both tested); `filter.tag` renders none; byte-verbatim from storage; receipts unchanged. Prior art: the recall lane-route and receipt suites.
  3. **Migration job**: atomic batch with in-transaction field clearing; unresolved content refuses cutover with fields UNCLEARED; anchor re-sourcing through the member index with unanchorable load-bearing claims going to unresolved; job lifecycle states + bounded retry + idempotent re-run; `origin=backfill` on every seeded row; per-task phase-2 cutover gating; a legacy corpus fixture.
- **Payload cap (peer rounds 2-3: pinned, units and retry semantics exact)**: the terminal commit's impression payload is capped at 256 KiB of UTF-8 SERIALIZED PAYLOAD BYTES (not JS chars, not token estimates — CJK and JSON escaping diverge all three); provisional at ≥4× today's largest real cross-task shape; the wire's 16 MiB envelope cap is a pathology bound, not a product bound. Overflow: DETERMINISTIC transaction rejection — never truncation, never a split commit — and because an identical retry cannot self-heal a deterministic failure, the rejection routes into the EXISTING deterministic-failure discipline: the retry budget applies only to REGENERATION, and regeneration may only COMPRESS prose and drop non-essential claims (peer round-4 finding 3) — it may NOT omit a touched lane's judgment nor demote a semantically-required replace to retain (an invalidated anchor or a STALE-clearing rewrite is required regardless of payload pressure); if every required replacement at its minimal legal form still overflows, the job fails operator-visible — with split commits rejected, there is no third path. The measurement gate validates the constant against the real maximum JOB touched set (cross-task HEADs included — one task's 33 lanes is not the job bound) and may adjust the CONSTANT, never the mechanism.
- **Acceptance gates that are experiments (peer's standing verdict)**: (a) corrected-C regeneration under the final teaching+validator with ZERO state inflations, INCLUDING a line-1-only reader arm (peer round-5 finding 3): a blind reader given ONLY each lane's line 1 must hold identity/current-law/state/frontier — a passing full impression never excuses a failing global line; (b) a state-inflation audit over one real regenerated segment (every claim's state vs anchors); (c) the worst-case maintenance payload measured at the real production maximum JOB touched set, with the linear-scaling statement and the cap constant validated; (d) the generation-acceptance delivery case (b-class above); (e) the migration-path scale gate (the largest legacy-field task, open or closed, through the backfill job).
- Good tests assert transaction outcomes and rendered output, never writer internals.
